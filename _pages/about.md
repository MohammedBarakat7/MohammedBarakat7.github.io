Mohammed Barakat is an incoming PhD student in Mechanical Engineering at Carnegie Mellon University, starting August 2026, in the Visual Design and Engineering Lab (VDEL).

Mohammed graduated from the University of Texas at Tyler with a B.S. in Mechanical Engineering (GPA 3.95, five-time President's List, Outstanding Student in the College of Engineering), where his work spanned computational modeling, embedded systems, and AI-driven engineering design. He has been a member of ASME since August 2023 and served as President of the UT Tyler student section for the 2024-25 academic year. He was named a NASA / Texas Space Grant Consortium Scholar in 2025, and worked as an undergraduate research assistant and lab assistant in the College of Engineering.

His technical experience includes machine-learning-enhanced molecular dynamics simulation of nanoscale silicon deformation, machine learning applied to CO₂ sequestration and subsurface hydrogen storage, non-invasive ultrasound brain imaging, Raspberry Pi/Arduino-based embedded systems, CAD and simulation in SolidWorks and ANSYS, and full-stack development. His senior capstone project was a NICU sensory bonding device that he led as both team lead and software lead: a Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W-based system that simulates a mother's presence for a baby in the NICU incubator through sound, scent, and haptic touch, remotely activated via a Wi-Fi-connected mobile app. The project was [featured on KLTV](https://www.kltv.com/2026/04/14/ut-tyler-engineering-seniors-showcase-projects-companies/). He holds certifications in Machine Learning (DeepLearning.AI / Andrew Ng) and IBM Data Science, and is an Autodesk Inventor Certified User, among others.

Mohammed is fluent in Arabic and English, and a beginner in German. His long-term goal is to become an AI researcher whose systems are built to survive contact with physical reality.
